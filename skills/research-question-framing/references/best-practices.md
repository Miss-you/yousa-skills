## Adapted Principles

### Question Formulation Technique

Source: The Question Formulation Technique was created by Dan Rothstein and Luz Santana, co-founders of the Right Question Institute (rightquestion.org).

Useful points:

- Use a clear question focus.
- Generate questions before judging or answering.
- Improve questions by distinguishing open and closed forms.
- Prioritize questions against the objective.
- Reflect on what was learned and how questions will be used.

How this skill adapts it:

- The `topic` becomes a question focus.
- Divergent question generation happens before source reading.
- Open/closed conversion is used to produce both exploration questions and source-verifiable checks.
- Prioritized questions become the handoff scope.

### Stanford d.school "How Might We"

Useful points:

- Use questions to turn a perspective into actionable provocations.
- Good HMW questions should be meaningful, relevant, insightful, and nuanced.
- Multiple reframings reveal different directions before solution work starts.

How this skill adapts it:

- Ambiguous research topics are reframed from several perspectives: user, runtime, evidence, failure mode, and decision.
- The output is not ideation; it is an actionable research frame.

### Purdue OWL and Cornell Research Guides

Useful points:

- A topic should be narrowed before source collection.
- State the topic as a question.
- Identify main concepts and keywords.
- Decide what types of sources are needed before searching.
- Narrow or broaden based on source availability.

How this skill adapts it:

- Research briefs require key terms, scope, source types, and evidence criteria before a source-code SOP starts.

### MIT Problem Statement Guidance

Useful points:

- Naming a broad topic is not the same as defining a problem.
- A useful problem statement includes the problem/context, the method or approach, and the purpose/scope.

How this skill adapts it:

- The brief requires situation, boundaries, method of inquiry, and scope.

### The Craft of Research

Useful points from publisher summary:

- Research moves from questions to answers, and then to claims supported by evidence.
- Researchers should anticipate reader reservations.
- Trustworthy sources must be distinguished from quick web-search results.

How this skill adapts it:

- The evidence matrix forces each question to map to proof and non-proof.
- Counter-questions and negative checks anticipate weak conclusions.
